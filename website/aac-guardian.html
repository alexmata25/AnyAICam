Technology behind AAC Guardian

The analytics that make Guardian possible

AAC Guardian brings together well-established computer-vision techniques. The examples below show what each technique looks like in general. They come from public, openly licensed sources, are not AnyAiCam output, and Guardian’s own views will look different.

Detect

Object Detection

The foundation of Guardian. An object-detection model draws a box around each person, product, bag, cart or vehicle it finds in a frame and labels it. Everything later in the workflow starts from these detections.

In a store, that means knowing where shoppers are, which items or bags are present, and where they are in the camera view, frame after frame.

Example image: pedestrian detection. Derivative work by Indif from a photo by ll0zz, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons, re-encoded for web.

Track

Multi-Object Tracking (MOT)

MOT gives every detected person or object a consistent track as it moves, so “a person” in one frame becomes “the same person” across seconds and minutes, even in a crowd.

Example image: people tracking (LiDAR-based visualization). Saxotango, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons, resized.

Illustration
Track

Person Re-ID & Cross-Camera Tracking

Person Re-Identification (Re-ID) recognizes when a person seen on one camera is the same person who appears on another, usually from overall appearance such as clothing and build. Cross-Camera Tracking uses that match to keep one continuous story as the person moves from aisle to checkout to exit.

Illustration created by AnyAiCam to explain the concept. Not a product screenshot.

Track · Understand

Trajectory Analysis

Trajectory Analysis studies the path a person takes: where they went, how fast, where they lingered and whether they doubled back. Combined with zones, a path that skips the checkout on the way to an exit can become one meaningful signal.

Example image: people trajectories and an event zone (LiDAR-based visualization). Saxotango, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons, cropped and resized.

Understand

Pose Estimation / Human Pose Tracking

Pose Estimation finds body keypoints such as head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips and knees, and connects them into a skeleton. Tracking that skeleton over time shows how someone is moving: reaching, bending, turning away or moving a hand toward a bag or pocket.

Example image: skeleton tracking on a depth view. Sang1938, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons, cropped to the depth panel and resized.

Illustration
Understand

Human-Object Interaction (HOI)

HOI describes what a person is doing with an object, as a relationship: person, picks up, product or product, placed in, bag. For retail, these interactions are often more meaningful than any single detection.

Illustration created by AnyAiCam to explain the concept. Not a product screenshot.

Understand

Zone/ROI Analytics

Zones, or Regions of Interest (ROI), are areas drawn on a camera view, such as a high-value aisle, a fitting-room entrance, the checkout lanes or an exit. Guardian is designed to give the same action a different meaning depending on where it happens.

Example image: zone-based counting on a CCTV view. Retail Sensing Ltd, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons, re-encoded for web.

Sample Guardian alert

An alert that explains itself

A Guardian alert is designed to tell staff why they are being asked to look, where it is happening and what evidence exists, so the review takes seconds rather than a search through hours of video.

  • Plain-language reasons, not just “motion detected”
  • Every camera involved, in time order
  • Short clips and snapshots ready to watch
  • A risk level (such as Low, Elevated or High) to help prioritize, never a verdict
  • Clear actions: review, mark as resolved or escalate under store policy

Evidence & incident organization

Every event becomes an organized incident

Instead of scattered clips on separate cameras, AAC Guardian is designed to gather everything about an event into one incident record that can be reviewed, annotated and kept according to your retention settings.

Timeline

One incident timeline

Each moment from detection to exit, in order, with the camera and time for every step.

Evidence

Clips and snapshots together

Short clips around each key moment, and snapshots of the most relevant frames, linked from every camera involved.

Reasons

Why it was flagged

The signals that contributed to the event and its risk level, written so a reviewer can check them against the video.

Review

Reviewer notes and outcome

Who reviewed it, when, what they decided and any notes. Dismissed events are recorded too.

Search

Find past incidents

Look up incidents by date, camera, zone or outcome, and spot repeated patterns over time.

Share

Export for authorized use

Package an incident’s clips and timeline for authorized internal or legal use, under your store’s policies.

The human-review decision boundary

Guardian assists. People decide.

AAC Guardian identifies potential suspicious activity and organizes the evidence. It does not determine that anyone committed theft. Every alert is a request for a person to review, and every decision and action stays with your staff and your store’s policies.

Guardian is designed to

  • Flag activity that may deserve a closer look
  • Follow the related person and event across cameras
  • Explain why an event was flagged and how strongly
  • Gather clips, snapshots and a timeline into one incident
  • Notify the right staff and record their review

Guardian does not

  • Decide that a person committed theft or any other offense
  • Accuse, confront, stop or detain anyone
  • Take enforcement action on its own
  • Replace the judgment of trained staff
  • Treat a risk level as proof
Responsible use: retailers remain responsible for using video analytics lawfully, including signage, privacy notices, retention and local rules on surveillance and loss-prevention practices. False alerts are possible with any analytics system, which is why every Guardian alert goes to human review.
